Architectural Philosophy & Patient Data Privacy
Why healthcare systems prioritize medical-grade encryption, data residency, and real-time telehealth stability.

Singapore healthcare platforms (across public restructured hospitals and private telehealth providers) must adhere strictly to the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) and Ministry of Health (MOH) cybersecurity guidelines. Architectures require end-to-end encryption for electronic medical records (EMR), localized data residency, and fault-tolerant WebRTC signaling servers for virtual consultations.

End-to-End Activity Walkthrough: Telehealth Video Consultation & EMR Update
Tracing how a secure video session connects doctor and patient while updating clinical notes.
Layer 1: Secure Patient Portal & WebRTC Signaling

What Happens: Patients authenticate via Singpass or biometric login. The client app negotiates peer-to-peer video/audio streams through TURN/STUN media servers encrypted via DTLS/SRTP.

Accountable Role: WebRTC / Telehealth Infrastructure Engineer 🏷️

Layer 2: Clinical Microservices & API Gateway

What Happens: Appointment scheduling, e-prescription routing, and doctor availability services handle business workflows behind a secure API gateway enforcing strict role-based access.

Accountable Role: Backend Clinical Systems Engineer 🏷️

Layer 3: Data Privacy & Access Governance Layer

What Happens: Every chart view or medication order is checked against patient consent records and logged into tamper-evident compliance audit systems to satisfy MOH regulations.

Accountable Role: Data Privacy & PDPA Compliance Officer 🏷️

Layer 4: Encrypted EMR & Database Vault

What Happens: Clinical notes and DICOM imaging files are stored in localized database clusters with column-level encryption at rest and multi-factor key management.

Accountable Role: Healthcare Database & Data Platform Engineer 🏷️
