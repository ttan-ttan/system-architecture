Architectural Philosophy & IoT Scale
Why logistics networks require real-time geospatial processing, edge computing, and resilient event streaming.

In Singapore's global logistics and transshipment hub, architectures must process millions of continuous GPS telemetry pings from vessels, trucks, and warehouse IoT sensors. Systems prioritize low-latency stream processing, geospatial indexing, and real-time inventory synchronization across distributed nodes.

End-to-End Activity Walkthrough: Real-Time Fleet Telemetry & Route Optimization
Tracing how GPS pings are ingested, processed, and used to recalculate delivery routes dynamically.
Layer 1: IoT & Telemetry Ingestion Gateway

What Happens: Connected vehicles and warehouse scanners stream lightweight MQTT or HTTP telemetry packets continuously into cloud ingestion endpoints.

Accountable Role: IoT Platform / Network Engineer 🏷️

Layer 2: Stream Processing & Geospatial Routing Engine

What Happens: Stream processing frameworks (Apache Flink / Kafka Streams) ingest location pings, matching coordinates against geospatial boundaries and traffic congestion maps in real time.

Accountable Role: Stream Processing / Backend Engineer 🏷️

Layer 3: Warehouse Management System (WMS) Microservices

What Happens: Inventory status, automated sorting systems, and dispatch triggers update asynchronously, ensuring warehouse stock levels match transport availability.

Accountable Role: WMS Software Engineer 🏷️

Layer 4: Supply Chain Data Lakehouse & Analytics

What Happens: Historical shipping routes, fuel consumption logs, and delivery lead times feed analytical data warehouses for predictive supply chain forecasting.

Accountable Role: Supply Chain Data Architect 🏷️
